Who we are

About Biblical Christian

Rooted in Scripture. Grounded in truth.

Biblical Christian is an independent, non-commercial educational library created to help readers understand the Christian faith through careful study of the Bible, engagement with church history, and thoughtful consideration of important questions.

Our purpose is not to replace the local church, the ministry of pastors, or personal study of Scripture. We hope to serve readers by making clear, substantial and accessible resources available to anyone who wants to examine what Christians believe and why.

Our approach

  • Scripture governs our teaching. We seek to interpret passages in context and compare Scripture with Scripture.
  • Christ is central. The person and saving work of Jesus Christ stand at the centre of the Christian faith.
  • Grace is the ground of salvation. Sinners are saved by God's grace through faith in Christ, not by the merit of their works. Good works are the fruit of salvation.
  • History matters. We learn from the faithful witness of Christians across the centuries while testing every tradition by Scripture.
  • Truth should be spoken honestly. We aim to represent other positions fairly, distinguish fact from interpretation, and correct errors when they are found.

Statement of Faith

The following statement sets out the principal biblical convictions that guide this ministry. It is intended to be a substantial summary of Christian doctrine, not a replacement for Scripture or a claim that every difficult question has been settled here.

1. The Holy Scriptures

We believe that the sixty-six books of the Old and New Testaments are the inspired Word of God. The Holy Spirit superintended the human authors so that, through their own words and literary styles, they wrote what God intended. Scripture is therefore true and trustworthy in all that it affirms, wholly sufficient as the supreme rule for what we are to believe and how we are to live, and authoritative over every human tradition, institution and opinion. The Bible is to be read according to its grammatical, historical and literary context, with attention to the unity of its message and its fulfilment in Jesus Christ. No private interpretation or teaching may overturn what God has spoken. The church is called to receive, teach, obey and proclaim the Scriptures, testing every doctrine by them (Psalm 19:7–11; Psalm 119:105, 160; Luke 24:27, 44–47; John 10:35; 2 Timothy 3:15–17; 2 Peter 1:19–21).

2. The one true and triune God

We believe there is one, and only one, living and true God: infinite, eternal, self-existent, unchanging, sovereign, holy, righteous, wise, good, loving and perfect in all his being. He alone is worthy of worship and possesses all authority over creation. The one God eternally exists in three distinct persons—the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit—who are each fully and equally God, sharing the one undivided divine essence. The persons are not three gods, nor are they merely three forms or roles of one person. The Father is God, the Son is God, and the Holy Spirit is God; yet there is one God. The three persons are equal in deity and glory, while acting in perfect unity in all their works (Deuteronomy 6:4; Matthew 3:16–17; 28:19; John 1:1–3; Acts 5:3–4; 2 Corinthians 13:14).

3. God's eternal purpose and providence

We believe God works all things according to the counsel of his own wise, holy and sovereign will. Before the foundation of the world, he graciously chose a people in Christ to be holy and blameless before him, not because he foresaw merit or worthiness in them, but according to his mercy and good pleasure. His purpose does not make him the author of sin, remove human responsibility, or excuse evil. God governs all things with perfect wisdom and righteousness, sustaining and directing his creation while creatures act according to their own desires and remain accountable for their choices. Nothing can frustrate his purpose, and his providence is a sure comfort to his people (Genesis 50:20; Proverbs 16:9; Isaiah 46:9–10; Acts 2:23; Romans 8:28–30; 9:10–24; Ephesians 1:3–14).

4. Creation and the purpose of humanity

We believe God created the heavens and the earth and everything in them, out of nothing, by his Word and for his glory. Creation is not eternal or independent of God; it exists because he made it and continues to sustain it. Human beings are created in God's image, male and female, with equal dignity and worth, and are called to know, worship and obey their Creator, care responsibly for his world, and live in love toward one another. Human worth does not depend on age, ability, status, ethnicity, wealth or usefulness. Marriage is God's good creation ordinance, a covenant union of one man and one woman, intended for lifelong faithfulness and reflecting, in its own way, the relationship between Christ and his church (Genesis 1:1, 26–28; 2:18–25; Psalm 24:1; John 1:3; Colossians 1:16–17; Revelation 4:11).

5. Humanity's fall and sin

We believe Adam and Eve were created upright, but through Adam's disobedience sin entered the world, bringing guilt, corruption and death. All people, apart from Christ, are sinners by nature and by their own actions. Sin is not merely a weakness or a lack of knowledge; it is rebellion against God's holy rule and affects every part of human nature—mind, will, affections and body. Every person is accountable to God and unable to overcome sin or restore fellowship with God by personal effort, religious observance or good works. Our greatest need is not simply improvement or instruction, but reconciliation with God and new life through Jesus Christ (Genesis 3:1–19; Psalm 51:5; Jeremiah 17:9; Romans 3:9–23; 5:12–21; Ephesians 2:1–3).

6. Jesus Christ, the Son of God

We believe Jesus Christ is the eternal Son of God, the second person of the Trinity, fully God and fully man in one person. He was conceived by the Holy Spirit and born of the virgin Mary. He lived in perfect obedience to the Father, fulfilling God's law without sin. He is the promised Messiah, the one mediator between God and humanity, and the only Saviour of sinners. In his earthly ministry he proclaimed the kingdom of God, demonstrated divine authority, and revealed the Father. He was crucified under Pontius Pilate, truly died and was buried. On the third day he rose bodily from the dead, appeared to many witnesses, and ascended bodily into heaven, where he reigns at the Father's right hand. He intercedes for his people and will return personally and visibly in glory (Isaiah 9:6; Matthew 1:18–23; Luke 1:26–35; John 1:1–14; Acts 1:9–11; Romans 8:34; 1 Corinthians 15:3–8; Philippians 2:5–11; Colossians 1:15–20; Hebrews 4:14–16).

7. The atoning death and resurrection of Christ

We believe that Jesus Christ willingly gave himself as the once-for-all sacrifice for sin. On the cross he bore the sins of his people, endured the judgment their sins deserved, satisfied divine justice, and reconciled them to God. His death was a real substitutionary sacrifice, not merely an example of love or an expression of solidarity with human suffering. By his blood he secured redemption, forgiveness and peace with God. His bodily resurrection is God's vindication of his Son, the declaration of his victory over sin and death, and the foundation of the believer's hope. Christ's finished work is fully sufficient; nothing can be added to it as a payment for sin (Isaiah 53:4–6, 10–12; Mark 10:45; Romans 3:24–26; 4:25; 5:8–11; 1 Corinthians 15:17–22; 2 Corinthians 5:21; Galatians 3:13; 1 Peter 2:24; 3:18; Hebrews 9:11–28; 10:10–14).

8. The person and work of the Holy Spirit

We believe the Holy Spirit is the third person of the Trinity, fully God, personal and active in the world. He inspired the Scriptures, bears witness to Christ, convicts the world concerning sin, righteousness and judgment, and brings sinners to spiritual life. He unites believers to Christ, indwells them, seals them for the day of redemption, assures them of their adoption, and empowers them for holiness, service and witness. He produces spiritual fruit in the lives of God's people and gives gifts for the building up of the church. The Spirit never contradicts the Word he inspired, and every claim to spiritual authority or experience must be tested by Scripture (John 14:16–17, 26; 15:26; 16:7–15; Acts 1:8; Romans 8:9–16; 1 Corinthians 12:4–11; Galatians 5:16–25; Ephesians 1:13–14; 4:30).

9. Salvation by grace

We believe salvation is entirely the work of God's grace, accomplished by Jesus Christ and applied by the Holy Spirit. God saves sinners not because of foreseen faith, moral achievement, religious effort or any worthiness in them, but because of his mercy and love. Those whom the Father has given to the Son will come to Christ, and Christ will lose none of them. The gospel must be proclaimed freely and sincerely to all people, calling everyone everywhere to repent and believe in Jesus Christ. Everyone who comes to Christ in faith will be received; no sinner who trusts in him will be turned away (John 6:37–40; Acts 13:48; Romans 8:29–30; 9:15–16; Ephesians 1:3–14; 2:4–10; 2 Thessalonians 2:13–14).

10. The new birth and effectual calling

We believe that because sinners are spiritually dead and unable to make themselves alive toward God, the Holy Spirit must give them new birth. In regeneration God renews the heart, gives spiritual life, and enables the sinner to respond to the gospel with repentance and faith. This work is God's gracious action, not a reward for a decision or an achievement of human will. Through the gospel, God calls sinners to Christ; by the Spirit's powerful work, those whom he calls are brought willingly to trust in Christ. This does not mean that people are saved apart from hearing and believing the gospel, but that their hearing and believing are themselves the result of God's gracious work in them (Ezekiel 36:25–27; John 1:12–13; 3:3–8; 6:44, 65; Acts 16:14; Romans 8:30; 1 Corinthians 2:10–14; Ephesians 2:1–5; James 1:18; 1 Peter 1:3, 23).

11. Repentance and faith

We believe that repentance and faith are inseparable gifts of God's grace and the proper response to the gospel. Repentance is a sincere turning from sin to God, involving a change of mind and heart that bears fruit in a changed direction of life. Faith is receiving and resting upon Jesus Christ alone for salvation, trusting his person and finished work rather than our own goodness or efforts. Neither repentance nor faith is a meritorious work or a payment for sin. We are not justified by the strength, sincerity or completeness of our repentance, but by Christ alone, received through faith alone. Yet the faith that receives Christ does not remain alone: it is accompanied by repentance and begins to produce obedience. Christians continue to repent and believe throughout their lives (Mark 1:14–15; Acts 2:37–38; 20:21; Romans 4:4–8; 10:9–17; 2 Corinthians 7:10; Ephesians 2:8–9; Philippians 1:29; 1 Thessalonians 1:9–10).

12. Justification, adoption and union with Christ

We believe God justifies sinners by declaring them righteous in his sight, forgiving their sins and accepting them as righteous, solely on the basis of Christ's obedience and atoning death. This righteousness is received through faith, not earned by works or infused as a basis for acceptance. God does not justify us because our faith is a good work, but because faith receives Christ, whose righteousness is our only ground of acceptance. All who are united to Christ by faith are also adopted into God's family. They are no longer condemned but are welcomed as God's children, given access to him as Father, and made heirs of his promises. Justification is God's gracious verdict and is not gradually earned or improved by our performance (Romans 3:21–28; 4:1–8; 5:1–2, 9; 8:1, 14–17; Galatians 2:16; 3:26; 4:4–7; Philippians 3:8–9).

13. Sanctification and good works

We believe everyone who is justified is also being sanctified by the Holy Spirit. Sanctification is God's gracious work of setting believers apart for himself and progressively making them more like Christ. It involves the believer's real effort, dependence on the Spirit, use of God's appointed means, and daily struggle against sin. Christians will not reach sinless perfection in this life, but they must not make peace with sin. Good works are the necessary fruit and evidence of living faith, prepared by God for his people to walk in; they are never the ground, instrument or price of justification. We are saved by grace for a life of obedience, love, mercy, purity and service (John 17:17; Romans 6:1–14; 8:13; 1 Corinthians 6:11; 2 Corinthians 3:18; Galatians 5:16–25; Ephesians 2:8–10; Philippians 2:12–13; 1 Thessalonians 4:3–8; Titus 2:11–14; James 2:14–26).

14. Perseverance and assurance

We believe all whom God has truly regenerated and united to Christ will be kept by his power and will persevere in faith to the end. Their perseverance does not rest on the strength of their own resolve, but on God's unchanging purpose, Christ's intercession and the Spirit's preserving work. Believers may fall into serious sin and experience seasons of doubt, weakness or spiritual darkness, but God disciplines his children, renews them to repentance and does not finally abandon them. The assurance of salvation rests first on God's promises in Christ and is strengthened by the inward witness of the Spirit and the visible fruit of a growing Christian life. This confidence is not permission to live carelessly; it encourages holiness, gratitude and steadfast faith (John 10:27–30; Romans 8:28–39; Philippians 1:6; 1 Peter 1:3–5; 1 John 2:3–6, 19; 5:11–13; Jude 24–25).

15. The universal church and local churches

We believe the universal church is the one people of God, consisting of all who have been redeemed by Christ from every nation, tribe, people and language. Christ alone is the head of the church, which is his body and his bride. The local church is a gathered community of professing believers who worship God, submit to the authority of Scripture, proclaim the gospel, teach the whole counsel of God, practise the ordinances, care for one another, and pursue holiness and unity. Christians are not to live in isolation but are called to commit themselves to a faithful local congregation. Every local church is accountable to Christ and his Word; no church, pastor, tradition or institution possesses authority equal to Scripture. Churches should seek peace and cooperation with other faithful churches while maintaining biblical convictions (Matthew 16:18; Acts 2:41–47; 14:21–23; 20:28; 1 Corinthians 12:12–27; Ephesians 1:22–23; 2:19–22; 4:1–16; 5:25–27; Colossians 1:18; Hebrews 10:24–25).

16. Church membership, leadership and discipline

We believe local church membership should reflect a credible profession of faith in Christ and a willingness to follow him in obedience. The church is called to receive believers, shepherd them, encourage their growth, and lovingly correct those who persist in serious and unrepentant sin. Biblical church discipline aims at the honour of Christ, the purity of the church, the restoration of the sinner and the protection of the congregation; it must be exercised with humility, patience, fairness and submission to Scripture. Christ has appointed qualified elders or pastors to teach and shepherd the church, and deacons to serve its practical needs. The qualifications for the office of elder or overseer given in Scripture are to be honoured. Elders must lead willingly and as examples, not domineering over those entrusted to them. The congregation also has responsibilities in recognising leadership, preserving sound doctrine and carrying out the life of the church (Matthew 18:15–20; Acts 6:1–6; 14:23; 20:17, 28; 1 Corinthians 5:1–13; Galatians 6:1; 1 Timothy 3:1–13; 5:17–22; Titus 1:5–9; 1 Peter 5:1–5).

17. Baptism and the Lord's Supper

We believe the Lord Jesus gave two ordinances to his church: baptism and the Lord's Supper. Baptism is for those who personally profess repentance toward God and faith in the Lord Jesus Christ. It is to be administered by immersion in water in the name of the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit, publicly portraying the believer's union with Christ in his death, burial and resurrection. Baptism does not earn salvation or regenerate by the mere performance of the act; it is an act of obedience and a visible confession of faith. The Lord's Supper is a regular act of remembrance and proclamation in which the gathered church eats bread and drinks the cup in obedience to Christ, proclaiming his death until he comes. In the Supper believers examine themselves, remember Christ's sacrifice, express their unity in him and look forward to his return. The bread and cup remain signs; Christ's sacrifice is not repeated, and the Supper is not another offering for sin (Matthew 28:18–20; Acts 2:38–42; 8:12, 36–39; Romans 6:3–5; 1 Corinthians 10:16–17; 11:23–29; Colossians 2:12).

18. Worship and the Lord's Day

We believe God alone is worthy of worship, and he must be worshipped in spirit and truth according to his revealed Word. The gathered church should devote itself to the reading and preaching of Scripture, prayer, singing, the ordinances, giving, fellowship and mutual encouragement. Worship is not to be shaped by human pride, entertainment or the desire to impress, but by reverence, joy, gratitude and love for God. We believe the first day of the week is the Lord's Day, the day on which Christ rose from the dead, and that Christians should ordinarily gather for worship and devote the day to the Lord's honour, spiritual good and works of necessity and mercy (John 4:23–24; Acts 2:42; 20:7; 1 Corinthians 14:26, 40; Ephesians 5:18–20; Colossians 3:16–17; Hebrews 10:24–25; Revelation 1:10).

19. The Christian's calling in the world

We believe Christians are called to love God with all their being and to love their neighbours as themselves. They are to pursue holiness, truthfulness, sexual purity, humility, justice, mercy, generosity, forgiveness and peace. They should honour lawful authority, work faithfully, care for the vulnerable, seek the good of their communities and refuse to repay evil for evil. Christians must obey God rather than people whenever human commands contradict God's Word. The church must not confuse the gospel with political power, national identity or any earthly ideology. Believers are to live as citizens of heaven while doing good in the world, bearing witness to Christ through both their words and conduct (Matthew 5:13–16, 43–48; 22:37–40; Romans 12:9–21; 13:1–7; Galatians 6:9–10; Ephesians 4:17–32; Philippians 3:20; 1 Peter 2:11–17).

20. The gospel and the mission of the church

We believe the good news of the gospel is that the holy God saves sinners through the life, death and resurrection of Jesus Christ. All people are commanded to repent and believe this good news. There is no other name by which we must be saved, and no other mediator between God and humanity. The church is commissioned to make disciples of all nations, baptising them and teaching them to obey everything Christ commanded. This mission includes the faithful preaching of the gospel, the planting and strengthening of churches, the instruction of believers, and practical love toward those in need. We should speak the truth with gentleness, courage and compassion, trusting God to give the growth (Matthew 28:18–20; Luke 24:46–49; John 14:6; Acts 4:12; Romans 1:16–17; 10:9–17; 1 Corinthians 15:1–4; 2 Corinthians 5:18–20; 1 Peter 3:15).

21. Angels, Satan and spiritual opposition

We believe God created the angels as spiritual beings to serve him and carry out his purposes. Satan and the demons are created, fallen beings who oppose God, tempt humanity and seek to hinder the work of the gospel. Satan is neither equal to God nor beyond God's control. Christ has decisively triumphed over the powers of darkness through his death and resurrection, and their final defeat is certain. Christians are called to resist the devil by standing firm in the truth, putting on the armour of God, praying and remaining steadfast in Christ, not by fear, superstition or fascination with evil (Job 1:6–12; Psalm 103:20–21; Luke 10:17–20; John 8:44; Colossians 2:15; Ephesians 6:10–18; Hebrews 1:14; 1 Peter 5:8–9; Revelation 20:10).

22. Death, resurrection and the final judgment

We believe physical death is the consequence of sin and is not the end of human existence. At death, the bodies of believers return to dust while their souls are made perfect in holiness and enter the presence of the Lord, awaiting the resurrection. Those who die outside of Christ remain under judgment, awaiting the final resurrection and judgment. At the return of Christ, all the dead will be raised bodily: those who belong to Christ to everlasting life, and those who have rejected him to everlasting punishment. God has appointed Jesus Christ to judge the living and the dead. Every person will give an account to God, and his judgment will be perfectly righteous. The hope of believers is not escape from creation but the resurrection life God has promised (Daniel 12:2; John 5:25–29; Acts 17:30–31; 24:15; 1 Corinthians 15:20–26, 42–57; 2 Corinthians 5:1–10; Philippians 1:21–23; Hebrews 9:27; Revelation 20:11–15).

23. The new heavens and the new earth

We believe God will bring his redemptive work to its glorious completion. He will make all things new, remove every trace of sin, sorrow, suffering and death, and dwell with his redeemed people forever. The new creation will be marked by righteousness, peace and the unveiled presence of God. Those who belong to Christ will see him, worship him and enjoy him without end. Those who refuse God's mercy in Christ will face the just and everlasting punishment of God. This promised future gives believers hope in suffering, courage in witness and motivation for faithful living now (Isaiah 65:17–25; Romans 8:18–25; 2 Peter 3:10–13; Revelation 21:1–8, 22–27; 22:1–5).

24. Christian unity and the truth

We believe all who are united to Christ by true faith belong to his one church, and Christians should pursue sincere love, humility, patience and unity with one another. This unity must be grounded in the truth revealed in Scripture and must never require us to deny the gospel or treat false teaching as harmless. We distinguish between the essential truths of the faith, important doctrines that shape the life and order of the church, and matters on which faithful Christians may disagree. We are to contend earnestly for the faith once delivered to the saints while speaking the truth in love, being ready to correct our own errors and refusing needless quarrels (John 17:17–23; Romans 14:1–19; Ephesians 4:1–6, 11–16; Philippians 2:1–5; 2 Timothy 2:24–26; Jude 3).

Our commitment

This statement summarises the convictions that guide the material published here. Individual articles may address disputed questions in greater detail. We welcome careful examination, correction from Scripture, and respectful discussion. The Bible itself—not this website—is the final authority.

Biblical Christian is an educational resource and is not a substitute for the teaching, fellowship, pastoral care and ordinances of a faithful local church.

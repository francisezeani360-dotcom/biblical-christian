How to Study the Bible in Context

Faithful Bible study begins by listening carefully to what God has said. A few sound habits can help us avoid reading our own ideas into a passage and learn to understand Scripture in its context.

Begin with prayer and humility

Ask God for wisdom and a willing heart (Psalm 119:18; James 1:5). Prayer does not replace careful reading. It expresses our dependence on the God who speaks through his Word.

Read the whole passage

Do not begin with a single sentence detached from its setting. Read the paragraph, the chapter and, when necessary, the whole book. Notice who is speaking, who is being addressed, what question or problem is being discussed, and how the passage fits into the argument.

Observe before interpreting

Look for repeated words, contrasts, commands, promises, reasons, connections and changes in the subject. Ask what the text actually says before deciding what it means. Write down questions rather than rushing to an answer.

Understand the context

Consider the historical setting, the type of writing, the author's purpose and the place of the passage in the book. Poetry, narrative, prophecy, wisdom, letters and apocalyptic visions use language in different ways. Read each according to its literary form.

Compare Scripture with Scripture

Clear passages help us understand more difficult ones. Consider how a passage relates to the rest of the Bible and to the unfolding story of creation, fall, redemption and new creation. The Old Testament's promises and patterns find their fulfilment in Christ, but avoid forcing a connection that the text itself does not support (Luke 24:25–27, 44–47).

Use study tools wisely

Several translations, a Bible dictionary, a concordance and trustworthy commentaries can be helpful. Learn to distinguish the biblical text from an author's explanation. No commentary, teacher, tradition or website has authority equal to Scripture. Check claims against the passages being discussed.

Apply the passage faithfully

Ask what the passage teaches about God, humanity, sin, Christ, faith and obedience. Consider its original meaning before asking how it applies today. Application should grow from the text rather than from a motivational idea attached to it.

Study with the church

God has given teachers to equip his people (Ephesians 4:11–16). Discuss Scripture with mature believers, listen to faithful preaching, and remain part of a local church. Personal study matters, but Christians are not meant to grow in isolation.

A simple set of questions

  1. What does the passage say?
  2. What did it mean in its original setting?
  3. How does it fit within the whole Bible and point to Christ?
  4. What does it teach me to believe, reject, love or do?

Did Jesus Really Rise from the Dead?

The resurrection is not an optional detail added to Christianity. It is at the heart of the apostolic message. This introduction considers the central historical claims and why Christians believe Jesus rose bodily.

The claim at the centre

The earliest Christian message was not simply that Jesus' teaching lived on. The apostles proclaimed that the man who was crucified had been raised from the dead (Acts 2:22–36; 1 Corinthians 15:3–8). Paul says that if Christ has not been raised, Christian preaching is empty and faith is futile (1 Corinthians 15:14–19).

The central historical questions

Any serious examination should consider several connected claims: Jesus was crucified and died; his followers soon proclaimed that he had risen; the disciples believed they encountered him alive; and this conviction transformed a frightened group into public witnesses. The New Testament also reports that the tomb was found empty. These claims must be examined in their literary, historical and cultural setting rather than reduced to slogans.

The sources are not identical in every detail, and historians debate how best to explain particular aspects of the accounts. A fair case should acknowledge those discussions rather than pretending there are no questions. At the same time, differences in emphasis do not automatically make the central testimony contradictory.

Considering alternative explanations

Proposals such as deliberate deception, hallucination, mistaken identity or the gradual development of a legend each need to explain the full range of evidence, including the early proclamation, the disciples' conviction and the shape of the resurrection narratives. It is not enough to name a possible explanation; it must account for the relevant data and be weighed against competing explanations.

History and faith

Historical investigation can assess testimony and events, but Christian faith also recognises the resurrection as God's act. The apostles did not present it as a private spiritual experience. They proclaimed that God raised Jesus, vindicating him and inaugurating the resurrection hope promised in Scripture (Acts 17:30–31; 1 Corinthians 15:20–28).

The resurrection does not merely show that Jesus was an inspiring teacher. It declares him to be the risen Lord. The appropriate response is to turn to God and trust in Christ, whose death and resurrection are the foundation of the gospel (Romans 4:24–25; 10:9–13).

Read the primary testimony

Begin with Mark 15–16; Luke 23–24; John 19–21; Acts 2:22–36; and 1 Corinthians 15:1–28. Read each account in full and consider the claims being made.

The most important message

You Need Jesus: The Good News That Can Save You

You were made to know God. Your sin has separated you from him. But God has done what you could never do for yourself: he has given his Son, Jesus Christ, to save sinners. Here is what that means for you.

A personal explanation of the Christian gospel · Biblical Christian

Before you read anything else, please understand this: Christianity is not first about becoming a better person, joining a religious organisation, or learning how to behave. It is about being reconciled to the living God through Jesus Christ.

You may have grown up in church. You may know Bible stories, attend services, pray sometimes, or consider yourself a good person. Or perhaps you have little religious background and are simply trying to understand what Christians believe. Wherever you are coming from, the question is the same: Are you right with God?

This is not a small question. Your life will not last forever. One day you will stand before the God who made you. The good news is that you do not have to face him trusting in your own goodness. God has provided a Saviour. His name is Jesus.

1. You were made by God, and your life belongs to him

You are not an accident without purpose. God created the heavens and the earth, and he made human beings in his image (Genesis 1:1, 26–27). That means your life has dignity and meaning. You were made to know your Creator, love him, trust him, worship him, and live in the world he made under his good authority.

God is not simply a more powerful version of us. He is holy, perfectly good, just, wise, and completely pure. He does what is right and never does evil. His goodness is not measured by our standards; he is the standard of goodness. He has every right to rule the lives he has made.

We often want the benefits of God's world while resisting God's rule. We want to decide for ourselves what is right, what matters, and how we should live. The Bible calls this rebellion against God sin.

2. Our greatest problem is sin

Sin is more than making mistakes or having a few bad habits. It is our failure to love God as he deserves, our refusal to obey his commands, and the wrong desires and actions that flow from hearts turned away from him. We sin in what we do, what we say, what we think, and what we fail to do.

Be honest with yourself. Have you ever lied? Have you taken what was not yours, even if it seemed small? Have you hated someone, held on to bitterness, used another person, looked with lust, spoken cruelly, or acted selfishly? Have you ignored God, taken his gifts for granted, or lived as though you were the highest authority in your life?

God's law does not exist to flatter us. It exposes us. We may compare ourselves with people who seem worse and feel reasonably good about ourselves. But the question is not whether we are better than someone else. The question is whether we have loved and obeyed the perfectly holy God.

“For all have sinned and fall short of the glory of God.”Romans 3:23

The Bible says that sin has affected every one of us. None of us can honestly claim to be innocent before God. Even our best deeds cannot erase the wrong we have done or turn us into people who have never sinned. We cannot make our record clean by trying harder from this moment onward.

And sin is serious because God is just. He does not pretend evil does not matter. He will judge the world in righteousness. The Bible describes the consequence of sin as death and separation from God (Romans 6:23). This is not meant to make you despair without hope. It is meant to help you see why you need a Saviour. A disease cannot be treated properly until we stop pretending it is harmless.

3. You cannot save yourself

Perhaps your response is: “I will change. I will become religious. I will do more good than bad. I will pray, give to the poor, attend church, and try to live a decent life.” Those things may have their proper place, but they cannot pay for your sins or make you righteous before God.

Imagine standing in a courtroom guilty of a serious crime. Telling the judge that you have also done some good things would not make justice disappear. In the same way, our good deeds cannot cancel our guilt before the God who knows everything. We cannot bargain with him, impress him, or earn our way into his favour.

Religion itself cannot save you. You can know Christian language and remain far from Christ. You can attend church every week and still trust in yourself. You can be baptised, know hymns, read Christian books, or come from a Christian family, yet never personally turn to Jesus in faith. These things must not become substitutes for the Saviour.

Nor can you rescue yourself by making a promise to become perfect. You need more than advice, motivation, or a fresh start. You need forgiveness for your guilt, a new heart, and peace with God. You need God to save you.

4. God has provided the Saviour: Jesus Christ

Here is the heart of the good news: God did not leave sinners without hope. In love, the Father sent his Son, Jesus Christ, into the world to save his people from their sins (Matthew 1:21; John 3:16–17).

Jesus is not merely a wise teacher or a good example. He is the eternal Son of God who became truly human. He lived among us, yet he never sinned. He loved the Father perfectly and obeyed him completely. He showed compassion to the weak, welcomed the needy, confronted hypocrisy, taught with authority, and announced the kingdom of God.

Jesus did what we have failed to do. Where we have disobeyed, he obeyed. Where we have been unfaithful, he remained faithful. He is the righteous Saviour sinners need.

But Jesus did not come only to show us how to live. He came to give his life. The central event of the gospel is his death on the cross and his bodily resurrection from the dead.

5. At the cross, Jesus took the place of sinners

At the cross, Jesus was not simply suffering as a victim of human cruelty, though human wickedness was truly involved. He willingly gave himself as a sacrifice for sin. He bore the judgment that sinners deserve so that everyone who trusts in him may be forgiven and reconciled to God.

This is sometimes called substitution: Jesus stood in the place of his people. He was not punished for sins he had committed; he had none. He suffered for sinners. The innocent one gave himself for the guilty.

“He was pierced for our transgressions; he was crushed for our iniquities; upon him was the chastisement that brought us peace, and with his wounds we are healed.”Isaiah 53:5

At the cross, God's love and justice are not enemies. God does not save by pretending sin is unimportant. Sin is judged, and the Son willingly bears the penalty. God remains just, and he is the one who justifies the person who has faith in Jesus (Romans 3:23–26).

Do not think that Jesus was forced into this against his will. He said that he lays down his life willingly (John 10:17–18). The cross shows the seriousness of our sin and the astonishing love of God. The Saviour gave himself for sinners who could never have earned his kindness.

6. Jesus rose from the dead

Jesus was buried, but death did not hold him. On the third day he rose bodily from the grave. His resurrection was not merely the survival of his influence or a comforting story told by his followers. The risen Jesus was seen by his disciples, and the apostles proclaimed that God had raised him from the dead (1 Corinthians 15:3–8).

The resurrection means that Jesus truly conquered death. It confirms that his sacrifice was not a failure, that he is the Son of God, and that he is Lord. It also means that death will not have the final word for those who belong to him. Jesus is alive, reigning, and able to save.

“Christ died for our sins in accordance with the Scriptures, that he was buried, that he was raised on the third day in accordance with the Scriptures.”1 Corinthians 15:3–4

The gospel is not simply that Jesus offers helpful ideas. It announces something God has done in history: Christ died for sins and rose again. The risen Lord now commands all people everywhere to repent, and God has appointed a day when he will judge the world through Jesus (Acts 17:30–31).

7. What must you do to be saved?

Jesus' message was clear: “Repent and believe in the gospel” (Mark 1:15). The apostles likewise called people to believe in the Lord Jesus (Acts 16:31). Salvation is not something you earn by changing your behaviour, saying the right prayer, or making promises to God. It is a gift received by trusting in Jesus Christ.

Repentance: stop trusting in yourself

Repentance is often misunderstood. It does not mean that you must first clean up your life, turn away from every sin by your own strength, or ask God for forgiveness as though that request pays for your wrongdoing. Asking forgiveness is good, and a changed life follows salvation, but neither is the payment for sin. Only Jesus can pay for sin.

In the gospel call, repentance means a change of mind: you stop relying on yourself, your goodness, your religion, or anything else to make you right with God. You recognise that your former way of thinking was wrong and that you need a Saviour. Instead of trusting yourself, you turn to trust Christ. Repentance and faith belong together: you turn from false confidence and place your confidence in Jesus.

This does not mean sin is unimportant or that a Christian should carry on living however they please. A changed life, growing obedience, and a desire to leave sin behind are the results of God's saving work in a person. They are not the price you pay to receive it.

Faith: trust that Jesus has done what you could never do

To believe in Jesus is not merely to agree that he existed. It is to rely on him personally as the one who died for sins and rose again. You are trusting that his death is sufficient to pay for your sins completely. You are not adding your good deeds, religious activities, repentance, or future obedience to his finished work as part of the payment.

Imagine you have a debt you cannot pay, and someone pays the entire amount for you as a gift. How much of the debt remains? Nothing. You do not contribute a little money to make the gift effective. You receive what has already been provided. In a far greater way, Jesus bore the punishment sinners deserve. Trust him, rather than trying to pay for your own sins.

“Believe in the Lord Jesus, and you will be saved.”Acts 16:31

Salvation is God's gift, not a wage you earn. We are saved by grace through faith, not because of our works, so that no one can boast (Ephesians 2:8–9). Faith is not a good work that earns salvation; it is the empty hand receiving Christ. God grants repentance and faith, and everyone who believes in Christ is justified by faith alone. The entire credit belongs to God, not to the sinner (Acts 11:18; Philippians 1:29; Ephesians 2:8–9).

8. What happens when someone is saved?

When a sinner truly comes to Christ, God does not merely give that person a fresh religious identity. He brings that person into a new relationship with himself.

  • Your sins are forgiven. Your guilt is not ignored; Christ has paid for sin. There is no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus (Romans 8:1).
  • You are counted righteous before God. God justifies the one who trusts in Jesus. You are accepted not because you have achieved a perfect record, but because Christ's righteousness is the only ground of your acceptance.
  • You are reconciled to God. The hostility caused by sin is replaced with peace through Jesus Christ (Romans 5:1, 10–11).
  • You receive new life. God gives a new heart and begins changing your desires. Good works do not purchase salvation; they are the fruit of the new life God gives (Ephesians 2:10; Titus 3:4–7).
  • You become part of Christ's people. Christians are not meant to follow Jesus alone. They belong to his church, where they hear the Word, worship, receive pastoral care, serve, and encourage one another.
  • You have a sure hope. Jesus promises eternal life to his people. The Christian's confidence rests in Christ's faithfulness, not in the strength of their own performance (John 10:27–30).

This does not mean that a Christian never sins, never doubts, or never suffers. Christians still need daily repentance, forgiveness, encouragement, and growth. The difference is that they now belong to Jesus. Their lives are no longer centred on self-rule; they are learning to follow the Lord who loved them and gave himself for them.

9. You do not need a special prayer or a promise to change

There is no special wording you must repeat to make Jesus save you. Walking to the front of a church, raising your hand, being baptised, or repeating a prayer cannot pay for your sins. The question is not whether you have performed a religious action. The question is whether you are relying on Jesus Christ alone.

Nor must you first make yourself a better person before you can trust him. If you could make yourself good enough to stand before God, you would not need a Saviour. Jesus came for sinners. Come to him with the guilt you cannot remove and trust that his sacrifice is enough.

When someone truly trusts Christ, that person will begin to live differently. They will grow in love for God, obedience, and hatred of sin. But those changes are the result of being saved, not an additional payment or qualification that completes Jesus' work. We are saved by faith alone; the faith that saves does not remain alone but produces good works (Ephesians 2:8–10; James 2:17).

Jesus promises, “whoever comes to me I will never cast out” (John 6:37). Do not wait until you think you are worthy. Trust the Saviour who is worthy.

So, what will you do with Jesus?

You have heard the message. God made you. You have sinned against him. You cannot remove your guilt or rescue yourself. Jesus Christ, God's Son, died for sinners and rose again. He offers complete salvation to everyone who trusts in him.

So here is the question: are you trusting Jesus to take the punishment for your sins, or are you still relying on yourself? You cannot do both. If you are counting on being a good person, attending church, asking forgiveness, or making up for your past, you are still looking to something you do. None of those things can pay your debt. Jesus has already done what you cannot do.

Trust him. Rely on his death as the full payment for your sins and on his resurrection as God's declaration of his victory. You do not need to add anything to his work. You do not need to make a promise that you will never sin again. Come to him by faith.

If you are trusting Christ, tell a faithful Christian and seek a sound local church where the Bible is taught, the gospel is preached, and Christ is honoured. Read the Gospel of John. Speak honestly with a pastor or mature believer about your questions. Learn what it means to follow Jesus, and do not hide your faith.

A final question

If God asked you, “Why should I let you into heaven?”, what would you say?

The answer is not, “Because I have tried to be good,” or “Because I have asked for forgiveness,” or “Because I have promised to change.” None of those can remove the guilt of sin. The answer is: “Because Jesus Christ died for my sins. I am trusting in him alone.”

Do you believe that Jesus paid for your sins completely? Are you relying on him alone, rather than on anything you have done or could do?

If you are trusting Christ, tell a faithful Christian and seek a sound local church where the Bible is taught, the gospel is preached, and Christ is honoured. Begin reading the Gospel of John. Learn from other Christians and grow in the new life God gives. Good works matter deeply, but they are the fruit of salvation, never its purchase price.

10. Read the words of Jesus for yourself

If you want to know more, begin with the Gospel of John. Read a little each day and ask: Who does Jesus say he is? What does he say about sin, eternal life, and believing in him? What do I need to do in response? You can also read the following passages slowly and in context:

  • John 3:1–21 — Jesus explains the need to be born again.
  • Luke 18:9–14 — Jesus tells of a sinner who asks God for mercy.
  • Luke 19:1–10 — Jesus came to seek and save the lost.
  • John 10:1–30 — Jesus is the good shepherd who gives his life for his sheep.
  • Romans 3:9–28 — our guilt, Christ's redemption, and justification by faith.
  • Romans 5:1–11 — peace with God through Jesus Christ.
  • Ephesians 2:1–10 — salvation by grace, not by works.
  • 1 Corinthians 15:1–8 — the death and resurrection at the heart of the gospel.

You may still have questions. Read the passages, ask honest questions, and speak with a trustworthy Christian who will open the Bible with you. But understand the central invitation: Jesus Christ has done everything necessary to save sinners. Stop relying on yourself. Trust him alone.
